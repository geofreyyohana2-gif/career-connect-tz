📖 Teachings & Guidelines

Your CV Should Be Concise

Recruiters spend an average of 6–8 seconds scanning a CV. If your document is cluttered, wordy, or poorly structured, it will be rejected before you even get a chance to prove your worth...

Recruiters spend an average of 6–8 seconds scanning a CV. If your document is cluttered, wordy, or poorly structured, it will be rejected before you even get a chance to prove your worth. A concise CV is not about removing content — it's about eliminating noise and focusing on what matters most to the hiring manager.

Key principles:

  • One page is ideal – for early‑career professionals. Two pages max for senior roles.
  • Use bullet points – not paragraphs. Bullets improve scan‑ability.
  • Achievement‑focused – start each bullet with a strong action verb and include numbers where possible.
  • Tailor to the role – customise your CV for every application. Generic CVs are easily spotted.

Example: Instead of “Responsible for managing a team”, write “Led a team of 12, improving productivity by 25% over 6 months.”

Pro tip: Save your CV as a PDF to preserve formatting, and name the file professionally – e.g., John_Doe_CV_2026.pdf.

Visit our Resources section for free CV templates and checklists.

Tailor Your CV for Each Job

One‑size‑fits‑all CVs rarely work. Customising your CV for each role significantly increases your chances of securing an interview...

One‑size‑fits‑all CVs rarely work. Customising your CV for each role significantly increases your chances of securing an interview. Hiring managers can tell when you've taken the time to understand their needs.

How to tailor effectively:

  • Analyse the job description – highlight keywords, required skills, and responsibilities.
  • Mirror the language – use the same terminology the employer uses.
  • Prioritise relevant experience – place the most relevant roles and achievements near the top.
  • Add a “Key Skills” section – make it easy for the recruiter to see your fit at a glance.

Remember: A tailored CV shows initiative, attention to detail, and genuine interest in the role.

⭐ STAR Method

Structure your answers to behavioural questions with clarity and impact.

What is the STAR Method?

STAR is a framework for answering behavioural interview questions – questions that ask you to describe a past situation or experience...

STAR is a framework for answering behavioural interview questions – questions that ask you to describe a past situation or experience. It stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result.

S
Situation

Set the context – what was the challenge or circumstance?

T
Task

What was your specific responsibility or goal?

A
Action

What did you do? Focus on your contribution.

R
Result

What was the outcome? Use numbers if possible.

Example: “In my last role (Situation), our team was struggling to meet deadlines (Task). I introduced a new project management system (Action). Within 2 months, we improved delivery times by 30% (Result).”

Pro tip: Prepare 3‑5 STAR stories for different themes: leadership, problem‑solving, teamwork, conflict resolution, and adaptability.

How to Prepare STAR Stories

Not every experience fits neatly into STAR. Here’s how to identify the right stories and craft them effectively...

Not every experience fits neatly into STAR. Here’s how to identify the right stories and craft them effectively:

  • Review the job description – look for keywords like “leadership”, “communication”, “problem‑solving”.
  • Think of 3‑5 key accomplishments – what are you most proud of in your career?
  • Write each story using STAR – keep it concise: 1‑2 minutes per story.
  • Practice aloud – record yourself to check for clarity and confidence.

Remember: The best stories are specific, measurable, and relevant to the role.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even with a good story, small mistakes can weaken your answer. Avoid these common pitfalls...

Even with a good story, small mistakes can weaken your answer. Avoid these common pitfalls:

  • Vagueness – “I managed a team” vs. “I led a team of 8 on a $2M project.”
  • Forgetting the “R” – always state the result.
  • Rambling – keep your story focused and under 2 minutes.
  • Using “we” too much – make sure the interviewer knows your role.

Practice tip: Time yourself. A strong STAR answer should be 90‑120 seconds.

❓ Common Questions

Prepare for the most frequently asked interview questions with confidence.

Tell Me About Yourself

This is often the opening question. It sets the tone for the entire interview...

This is often the opening question. It sets the tone for the entire interview. Your answer should be a professional summary – not your life story.

Formula:

  • Present – what you’re doing now
  • Past – key experience or skills
  • Future – why you’re excited about this role

Example: “I’m currently a Marketing Coordinator at X. I’ve spent the last 3 years building campaigns and analysing data. I’m now looking to take on more responsibility in a leadership role, which is why I’m excited about this opportunity.”

What Are Your Strengths and Weaknesses?

This question tests self‑awareness and honesty. Here’s how to answer without sounding arrogant or insecure...

This question tests self‑awareness and honesty. Here’s how to answer without sounding arrogant or insecure:

Strengths:

  • Choose 2‑3 strengths that are relevant to the role
  • Back them up with examples

Weaknesses:

  • Choose a real but minor weakness
  • Explain what you’re doing to improve it

Example: “I sometimes struggle with public speaking, so I’ve joined a local Toastmasters club to build my confidence.”

Why Do You Want to Work Here?

This question tests your research and genuine interest in the company...

This question tests your research and genuine interest in the company.

How to answer:

  • Show you’ve done your homework – mention a recent project, value, or achievement of the company
  • Connect it to your career goals – explain why this role fits your path
  • Be specific – avoid generic answers like “I like your company culture”

Example: “I’ve followed your work in sustainable energy for years. Your commitment to innovation aligns with my background in renewable engineering, and I’d love to contribute to your next project.”

🔍 Research the Company

Know the company inside out – and show it.

What to Research Before an Interview

Thorough research is one of the most underrated interview preparation steps...

Thorough research is one of the most underrated interview preparation steps. It shows initiative and genuine interest.

  • Company website – mission, values, recent news, products
  • LinkedIn – look at your interviewer(s) and the team
  • Glassdoor – reviews, interview experiences
  • Competitors – understand the industry landscape
  • Social media – see what the company is posting

Pro tip: Prepare 2‑3 questions about the company to ask at the end of the interview. It leaves a strong impression.

🗣️ Body Language

Your non‑verbal cues speak louder than words.

Mastering Non-Verbal Communication

Body language can make or break an interview. Here’s how to project confidence...

Body language can make or break an interview. Here’s how to project confidence:

  • Eye contact – maintain natural eye contact (not staring)
  • Posture – sit up straight, lean slightly forward
  • Hand gestures – use them naturally to emphasise points
  • Smile – it builds rapport and warmth
  • Mirror – subtly match the interviewer’s energy

Watch out for: crossed arms, fidgeting, looking down, or a weak handshake.

✉️ Follow‑up

Leave a lasting impression after the interview.

The Art of the Follow‑up Email

A thoughtful follow‑up can set you apart from other candidates...

A thoughtful follow‑up can set you apart from other candidates. It shows professionalism and gratitude.

Email structure:

  • Subject: Thank You – [Your Name]
  • Paragraph 1: Thank the interviewer for their time
  • Paragraph 2: Mention something specific you discussed
  • Paragraph 3: Reiterate your interest and enthusiasm
  • Closing: Professional sign‑off

Timing: Send it within 24 hours of the interview.

Pro tip: If you interviewed with multiple people, send a personalised email to each one.

🎓 Scholarship Guidance

Your Roadmap to Funding

Practical strategies to find, apply for, and win scholarships – without the overwhelm.

📌 The 3‑Step Process

01
Identify

Find scholarships that match your profile, field, and level. Use databases, university portals, and government sites. Start early and cast a wide net.

02
Prepare

Gather transcripts, recommendation letters, and a strong personal statement. Tailor each application to the specific scholarship. Quality over quantity.

03
Submit

Double‑check requirements, meet deadlines, and follow up. A polished application stands out. Submit early to avoid last‑minute issues.

💡 Quick Tips

Write a Winning Personal Statement

Show your passion, connect your goals to the scholarship’s mission, and be authentic. Use specific examples, not generalities.

Get Strong Recommendations

Choose referees who know you well and can speak to your abilities. Give them context about the scholarship and your achievements.

Stay Organised

Create a spreadsheet of deadlines, requirements, and status. Submit early to avoid last‑minute issues.

Research Thoroughly

Look beyond the big names. Many smaller, niche scholarships have fewer applicants and higher success rates.

❌ Common Mistakes

  • ✕ Submitting a generic personal statement
  • ✕ Missing the deadline
  • ✕ Ignoring eligibility criteria
  • ✕ Not proofreading before submitting
